Technical Build Brief — for Jelani, Addis Coders
At-home beauty, grooming and massage marketplace for Addis Ababa  ·  Prepared by Addis Alemayehou  ·  September 2026  ·  Draft v1
1. What Konjo is
A two-sided marketplace app for Addis Ababa. Vetted beauty and wellness professionals (barbers, nail techs, braiders, makeup artists, massage therapists) list themselves; clients book them to their home, hotel or office, pay in-app, and rate the service. The business is owned and run by two founders, Tati and Sara; I am funding the build and early running costs. Target clients are expats, diaspora and professionals in Bole, Old Airport, CMC and Ayat. Launch target: November 2026, in time for the Genna/diaspora season.
What I'm asking of Addis Coders: a student team to design and build the product under your supervision, as a paid project. Konjo owns all code, data and IP; the students get a real shipped product on their CVs and a stipend.

3. Core flows the product must get right
Booking lifecycle: Client books → pro accepts (or auto-reassign to next nearest pro if no acceptance in 15 min) → pro checks in on arrival → service → pro checks out → client rates → payout queued.
Money: Client pays at booking. Funds are held by Konjo, not the pro, until check-out. Commission (18%) is deducted; the pro's share is paid weekly. Cancellation free before the pro starts the journey; after that the travel fee is forfeited. Client no-show: commission non-refundable.
Trust and safety: Pros go live only after admin approval. Clients verify phone (and ID for first massage booking). Female-therapist-only option. Live status and session timer during massage visits; SOS button on both sides that alerts admin and shares location. Ratings below 3.0 auto-hide a profile pending review.
4. Technical guidance
Stack (recommended, not mandatory): React Native (Expo) for the two mobile apps, Next.js for admin and mobile web, Node.js/NestJS or Python/FastAPI backend, PostgreSQL, Redis for queues, hosted on AWS or a comparable provider. If your students are stronger in another modern stack, propose it — the stack matters less than clean architecture and documentation.
Integrations: Telebirr and CBE Birr merchant APIs; a card gateway (Chapa or similar) for USD/diaspora cards; SMS gateway for OTP (Africa's Talking, or Ethio Telecom's); Google Maps for zones and addresses; push via Firebase.
Security and data: All personal data (IDs, addresses, phone numbers) encrypted at rest; role-based access; no ID images visible to clients; payment data never stored on our servers; HTTPS everywhere; rate limiting on OTP; a basic incident and data-deletion procedure documented.
Performance and locale: Works on mid-range Android and slow 4G; Amharic and English strings in a single i18n file from day one; app size kept small; offline-tolerant booking screens.
